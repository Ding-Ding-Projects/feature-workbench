Built-in authenticator

Keeps two-step sign-in codes for any account on this device, with copy, a countdown and the next code.

What it must do

  • Generate standard time-based codes (SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-512; 6 to 8 digits; any period) that match the published test vectors.
  • Enroll by setup link, QR image, clipboard, camera where available, or a typed key.
  • Show each code large with copy, a readable countdown and a peek at the next code; say plainly when the device clock is off.
  • Keep secrets on this device; ordinary exports leave them out and say so, and a secrets export needs two-key confirmation.

verifiedTest vectors for every hash at 6 and 8 digits, rollover, a skewed clock and restart persistence.

Independent per-element experience locks

Lets a person put a playful PIN, password or code lock on any single element, each with its own credentials.

What it must do

  • Offer "Lock this element" on every element, tab, tab group and appearance value, through a small anchored wizard.
  • Choose exactly one of six mixes: PIN, password, PIN and password, password and code, PIN and code, or all three.
  • Give each lock its own credentials, with no master key and no inheritance; a locked element stays disabled, and no shortcut, palette jump or automation gets around it.
  • Say on every lock that it is for fun, not security, and that clearing this site's local data resets it.

verifiedAll six mixes, keypad and typed PIN on one attempt budget, and shortcut and palette bypass attempts failing.

Clear pricing and no promotional nagging

Keeps the core free and any paid extra clearly priced, with no pop-ups asking for money or reviews.

QR authenticator pairing

Pairs an authenticator by scanning a QR code drawn on this device or typing the key, then confirms with one code.

What it must do

  • Generate the secret on this device and draw the QR locally, never through an online service.
  • Show the grouped key, algorithm, digits and period beside the QR, matching what it encodes.
  • Keep the QR dark-on-light with its quiet zone in both themes; reveal the key only on request and never write it to logs, exports or history.
  • Turn the factor on only after the person types back one current code.

Two-key destructive action confirmation

Holds back a destructive action until two keys are turned and a slider is pulled all the way across.

What it must do

  • Build it in the app's own interface, anchored beside the destructive control; never a hosted page or an outside challenge.
  • Name the exact action and the data it affects.
  • Require two independently turned keys before a full-range slider wakes; act only when the slider reaches the end.
  • Keep Emergency exit, Escape and Back always available; they do nothing and return focus.

Local fictional recovery desk

Gives a locked-out person a playful, entirely local help desk that points to the one real fix.

What it must do

  • Open the playful desk from the unlock prompt, the lock setting and Help.

  • Keep one plain line outside the joke, unchanged by the playfulness level: nothing is sent and nobody reads it.
  • Never imitate a real company's support branding or promise a reply time.

Lockout wait recovery activities

Lets a person shorten a lockout wait with quick activities, without ever skipping the credential.

What it must do

  • Offer the rungs in order: pick the dim sum dish, ten easy sums, whack-a-mole, then simply wait.
  • Clear only the waiting time, never the credential; used attempts stay used and escalation is unchanged.
  • Cap ladder rounds per rolling hour and grade each answer against a single-use challenge; timed rounds cannot end early.
  • In school mode, start at the sums with the dim sum rung left out.

verifiedEach rung won and lost, a replayed or expired challenge refused, and the hourly cap reached.
